# ACADEMIC WRITING STYLE GUIDE

**Objective:** Translation of scientific texts into British English (UK) whilst maintaining the author's personal style.

---

## 1. Spelling Conventions (British English)

### -ise/-isation Suffixes
Always use the British ending:
- `optimise` (instead of optimize)
- `characterise`
- `normalise`
- `synchronisation`
- `minimisation`

### -our Endings
- `behaviour` (instead of behavior)
- `colour`
- `favour`
- `neighbour`

### Double Consonants
- `labelling` / `labelled` (instead of labeling/labeled)
- `modelling` / `modelled`
- `travelling` / `travelled`
- `signalling` / `signalled`

### -re Endings
- `centre` (instead of center)
- `metre`
- `fibre`

---

## 2. Tone and Editorial Voice

### Person Balance
- Use **"We"** (first person plural) for design decisions, proposals, and own conclusions:
  - "In this work, we propose..."
  - "We found that..."
  - "We argue that..."
  
- Use the **passive voice** to describe standardised experimental procedures:
  - "The signals were recorded..."
  - "Data analysis was performed using..."
  - "Participants were recruited from..."

### Formality Level
- Maintain a **high technical register**
- **Avoid contractions**: use "do not" instead of "don't", "cannot" instead of "can't"
- **Avoid informal phrasal verbs**: 
  - Prefer `obtain` over "get"
  - Prefer `investigate` over "look into"
  - Prefer `examine` over "check out"
  - Prefer `commence` over "start up"
- Prefer **verbs of Latin origin** for their academic precision

## 3. Structure and Literary Cohesion

### Preferred Logical Connectors

**Causality:**
- "Therefore"
- "Consequently"
- "Hence"
- "Thus"
- "As a result"
- "Accordingly"

**Contrast:**
- "However"
- "Nevertheless"
- "Despite this"
- "On the other hand"
- "Notwithstanding"
- "Conversely"

**Addition:**
- "Moreover"
- "Furthermore"
- "In addition"
- "Additionally"
- "Besides"

**Sequence:**
- "Subsequently"
- "Following this"
- "In turn"

### Methodological Transitions
Begin sections with framing phrases such as:
- "To address this issue..."
- "Regarding the ..."
- "With respect to the experimental setup..."
- "In continuation with..."
- "Building upon this foundation..."

## 4. Recurring Technical Terminology

### HRI and Signals Context
- Always use **"Real-time"** (hyphenated when used as an adjective)
- **"Estimation"** or **"Assessment"** for emotion evaluation
- **"Robustness"** and **"Feasibility"** for system validation
- **"State-of-the-art"** for bibliographic references (hyphenated)
- **"Ground truth"** for reference data

### Data and Statistics

- "Mean value"

## 5. Notation and Acronym Format

### Acronyms
- Define the full acronym the **first time** and use the abbreviation thereafter
- Example: "Electroencephalogram (EEG)"
- Maintain acronyms in English even if the original text has them in Spanish

### Units
- Maintain a **space** between the value and the unit
- Examples: `250 Hz`, `10 ms`, `500 μV`

### Mathematical Notation
- Maintain original notation without translation
- Variables in italics

---

## 6. Section Structure

### For Abstracts/Introduction
1. **Context:** "Recent advances in [Field] have shown..."
2. **Problem/Gap:** "However, existing methods often lack..."
3. **Proposal:** "In this paper, we present a novel approach to..."
4. **Methodology:** "The proposed system was validated through..."
5. **Result/Impact:** "Our findings suggest that..."

### Paragraph Transitions
- Maintain coherence with the previous paragraph
- Use linking phrases such as:
  - "Building on this foundation..."
  - "In continuation with this analysis..."
  - "Extending this perspective..."

---

## 7. Citation Handling

- **Maintain original format:** (Author et al., 2023)
- Do not translate the titles of cited works
- Maintain author names without modification

---

## 8. Additional Notes

- Preserve original emphasis (italics, bold)
- Maintain heading structure (##, ###)
- Preserve lists and enumerations with their original format
- Do not add or remove content; translate faithfully
